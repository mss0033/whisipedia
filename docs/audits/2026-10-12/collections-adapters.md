## Questions answered

**Q1. What does the refine produce, and can a reader read it?**
- **Doc collections.** Text is the catalog header plus pdfium text of up to 400 pages, or the HTML or DOCX
  text. There are no `links`. The `html_ref` is `file:<gen>/<file>`, and nothing serves it (CA-12).
  - Text PDFs (FEMA, NIST, WHO, OSTI, CRS) are readable as plain text.
  - USGS topo maps, NOAA and NGA charts and image-only scans give only a 3-5 line header (title, issuer,
    edition, banner). The map itself cannot be reached from the reader.
  - Zip archives give member names.
  - CIA reading-room items are readable through the Archive's OCR text PDF (`prefer: text-pdf`).
- **Gutenberg.** Full text and a link to the illustrated ZIM page. Verified.
- **Open Library.** Catalog records only, by design. Links leave the library (CA-12).
- **PubMed.** Abstracts and MeSH; DOI and PMC links are external.
- **CourtListener.** Full opinion text.
- **Kiwix.** Rendered ZIM HTML (`zim:` html_ref).

**Q2. Fetch, screen and ingest.**
- Partial listings: CA-9, CA-10.
- Withdrawal inference: CA-8, CA-9, CA-10, CA-14.
- Resume: an MD5 reuse within the same cycle is correct. Same-version reruns: CA-15.
- Quarantine: CA-11, CA-16.
- Concurrency: the `_screen` thread confinement is correct. Throttle: CA-21.
- Resolve cache: CA-10. Listing cache: correct (partial listings are never cached).
- `true_suffix` and `well_formed`: CA-2.
- Zip and DOCX: CA-17.
- Image-only rules: correct, but they also admit error bodies (CA-2).
- The `published` rule makes every marking requirement it sits beside moot. That is by owner decision, but it
  is why CA-5 matters.
- Safety lists: CA-3 and CA-4 (both directions).

**Q3. Registry.**
- Sources without `rules:` silently ignore their filter files, and the review tool writes there anyway (CA-1).
- `formats` is unused (CA-11).
- Unknown keys are not caught (CA-20).
- The army fires and air-defence series are uncovered (CA-3).
