## Part 1. Gaps in what the library holds

The earlier report said the remaining problems were "mostly gaps in the archives themselves (some
missing images and files)". Measured properly, most of them were not upstream at all.

### 1.1 How the gaps were found

1. **Trawler recheck.** The trawler had logged 133 distinct missing files and dead links. Each was
   rechecked live and then looked up directly inside its archive: 66 of them were in the archive.
2. **Census** (`census.py` in the session scratchpad): for every ZIM source except the three largest
   (Gutenberg, Wikipedia, Stack Exchange, studied separately), up to 400 random HTML pages; each
   image and link resolved the way the API resolves it, then looked up in the archive (exact path,
   both slash forms, `index.html`). Also counted: robot-check pages saved in place of content, pages
   with no text, links that a site-root rule would fix.
3. **Following the nightly failure**: the 2026-10-17 index run was killed for memory; the cause was
   traced to one PDF.

## Part 2. Research round: new high-quality sources

### 2.1 Plan

1. **Coverage inventory.** What the library holds per domain, against the registry (194 entries).
2. **Kiwix catalog diff.** The live OPDS catalog against everything the registry selects; triage of
   the English and multilingual archives the library lacks.
3. **Blocked top-tier candidates.** Why each T1 candidate is still missing; unblock the cheap ones.
4. **Desk research** in thin domains (law, medicine, reference, preparedness, computing), with live
   checks of access, size and licence.
5. **Selection** by the existing rubric (licence and issuer authority as gates; quality, density,
   uniqueness, cost).
6. **Acquisition**: registry entries, a new adapter where none fits, verification after download.

### 2.2 Findings

**Kiwix catalog** (3,658 entries; 1,313 in English or multilingual; 1,198 not held). Most of the
volume is out of scope or redundant: TED and other video channels (the Navigator does not play
archive video yet), per-topic Wikipedia subsets (the full English Wikipedia is held), per-language
Gutenberg subsets (the multilingual archive is held), Stack Exchange (held under its own selector),
and 193 map archives (330 GB; they need a map viewer the Navigator does not have). Taken: the
archives below.

**Blocked T1 candidates.** US Code and the eCFR had no adapter; both publish complete official XML
for free (US Code: one 109 MB zip per release point; eCFR: 49 title files, 824 MB, updated daily).
Standard Ebooks' bulk downloads and OPDS feed now need a paid membership; its texts largely
duplicate Gutenberg's. OpenStax publishes no bulk channel; most of its books are in LibreTexts,
which the library holds. NEETS, Joint Publications, USMC and Army CMH stay blocked by
firewalled or missing official mirrors (research doc §5.6). Borden Institute and OSTI are being
fetched by the collection lanes.
