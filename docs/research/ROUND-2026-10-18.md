licence and issuer are gates; then quality, density, uniqueness and cost.

**Reanalysis (the past atmosphere on a grid).** WeatherBench 2 publishes ERA5 1959-2022 as Zarr
under the Copernicus licence (free, attribution): 64x32 6-hourly 67 GB, 240x121 (1.5°) about 390+ GB,
0.25° far larger. ERA5 at 64x32 and 1.5° gives the models below their inputs. NOAA-CIRES 20th Century
Reanalysis (1836-2015) is to check (its server refused a listing).

Each with its code (git snapshot) and documentation, so it runs offline on ERA5 from WeatherBench 2.

**Simulation (numerical weather and climate models, source and manuals):** WRF (public domain),
MPAS (BSD), the UFS Weather Model (NOAA, open), ICON (BSD-3 since 2024), MetPy and its docs.

**Teaching:** Stull, *Practical Meteorology* (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, 82 MB PDF; registered: `stull-practical-meteorology`); NWS JetStream (public
domain, online weather school).

**Modern weather.** An offline library keeps snapshots, not live feeds: the latest GFS and ECMWF open
data (CC BY 4.0) analyses as of each fetch are the "now" a model can start from.

## 6. What needs the owner

1. **NAS write budget.** Downloads into the vault share `[vault] ingest_bytes_per_sec` = 40 MB/s,
   a quarter of it while the library is in use: the basemap came in at ~8 MB/s. 12 TB at that pace
   takes months. A separate, higher budget for map downloads at night would make it weeks.
2. **American Stories** (a derived copy of Chronicling America, D11).
3. **Trove** API key and terms.
4. Licences still to read before taking: WeatherNext 2, NeuralGCM, Pangu-Weather and FuXi (likely
   non-commercial: allowed for a personal library, as for Stull's textbook).
